Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice

Restore Your Honor. Protect Your Master. Hesitation is Defeat.

Shura Mode - Demon Awakening

Shura is a demonic state capable of turning a warrior into a mindless, bloodthirsty killing machine. If you want to shred humanity within you, type "shura" below. Remember: actions have consequences!


Hesitation is defeat. Take a step wrong and be punished. Play as Wolf, a skilled shinobi that must protect his master, Kuro, at all costs. Or betray those who supported you in the course of the game and let the flames of Shura consume you, becoming a real demon. The choice is yours, and so is the consequences.

Swords

When you hear the word "Shinobi", you automatically think about sword. Here, is no different. Kusabimaru is your trusty companion, given by Kuro, the divine heir, the first time you see him. You can attack, deflect, use special attacks and combine its combos with your Prosthetic Tools, which is a topic we will address later in this page.

Kusabimaru is not the only sword in the game. In order to make Kuro's dream of becomming mortal become true, we must advance through the game. We must defeat Genichiro, enter Senpo Temple, reach the Main Hall, defeat the Folding Screen Monkeys and, after all the efforts, receive the Blade from the Divine Child who is in the Inner Sanctum.

With a cost of three Spirit Emblems, you can use the Mortal Draw attack. It is a high-damage attack that is excellent to break postures and tear enemies's HP. By pressing L1 + R1 on PlayStation, LT + RB on Xbox or Left Button + Right Button, Wolf will perform a extended-range slash. A more powerful version of the move can be unlocked in the skill tree, and is called Enpowered Mortal Draw.

Prosthetic Tools

There are a total of ten Prosthetic Tools Wolf can use, and each of them has its own upgraded versions. Some of them you can find across the map, others you can buy with some merchants. It is important to acknowledge that there is a chronological and more common order to find those tools, but it is easy to miss some.

Loaded Shuriken

This one is hard to miss. You can find the Loaded Shuriken in the Ashina Outskirts region, at the beginning of the game. Use your grappling hook to jump onto the roof or enter through a hole in the wall on the upper floor. Then, go back to the Dilapidated Temple and give it to the Sculptor to fit it onto your prosthetic arm.

This tool can one-shot weak enemies, like dogs, for example. In fact, before you find it, there is a group of dogs patrolling the area. It seems weak compared to other tools, but it is not the case. The shuriken is exceptionally good against agile enemies, like the Lone Shadows, an elite group of shinobi agents who serve the Interior Ministry.

Lady Butterfly, an optional boss from the Hirata Estate memory area, suffers against this tool, as it can easily interrupt many of her aerial attacks at the cost of just one Spirit Emblem. Its cost is low, but its versatility is quite unique. Never underestimate what a shinobi can do with his shurikens.

Shinobi Firecraker

Another Prosthetic Tool that is hard to miss. This one you cannot find exploring, you have to buy from a Merchant located in the Ashina Outskirts. Proceed past the area with the first mini-boss (General Naomori Kawarada), look to the left before passing though a gate with a cannon-wielding enemy and use your grappling hook on the elevated trees or stone structures. Follow the path up and you should find a small tent where you can purchase Robert's Firecrackers.

This tool is excellent against beast-type enemies, as they get scared easily with the sound. It makes their postures break easily, forcing a long flinch animation. Even Guardian Ape, one of the most memorable and hard bosses, gets scared by the sound of it. It can also stun human enemies, giving you a safe window to attack or heal.

At the cost of just one Spiritual Emblem, you can easily stun almost anything that crosses your path. Its versatiliy allows you to use the tool both ofensively and defensively, whether you want to attack the enemy or heal yourself. The Robert's Firecrackers Item, necessary to install the Prosthetic Tools, costs 500 sens, which is a low price. Go get some money and this Prosthetic Tool is yours. In order to achieve his goals, a good Shinobi is not afraid of distracting his enemies.

Flamevent

This one is, chronologically, the first Tool you can find within Hirata State memory. By progressing through the stage, you will find a campfire surrounded by a group of bandits and axe/katana guards. Clear out the enemies (or sprints/lure then away) and interact with the fire to loot the Flame Barrel. Then, you know what to do: go back to the Sculptor and he will build the Flame Vent using the Flame Barrel.

This Tool is extremely powerful. It drains vitality over time and prevents enemies from recovering posture, which is more important than the health bar in this game. It causes heavy flinching, panic and massive damage against Red-Eyes enemies, like the Chained Ogre and other beasts. With the Living Force skill, you can ignite your own Katana with fire. Here, fire frightens even the most terrifying enemies.

Unlike other Tools, and the ones i have mentioned before, this one costs three Spiritual Emblems. It is recommended to use it wisely, as it can drain your emblems pretty quick if you keep spamming. If timed poorly, it will leave vulnerable to enemy attacks. Being vulnerable in the world of Sekiro means you are dead, so try to use it properly, because this is a game-changing tool.

Loaded Axe

The Loaded Axe is, chronologically, the second Tools you can find within Hirata Estate. Its location lies close to the last one, where we found the Flame Vent. By progressing, you will find small courtyard on the right featuring a small garden shrine house guarded by two talking bandits/enemies. You can easily kill or lure them. Then, open the small shrine door and pick up the Shinobi Axe of the Monkey. Then, take the item to the Sculptor.

This tool is ridiculous. Its heavy-damage it is so brutal it can one-shot shield and hat enemies. This top-tier performance weapon deals both massive posture and vitality damage. Once it hits, the enemies will very likely feel the impact of your game-changer toy. Combining it with the Fang abd Blade skill lets you chain an axe smash directly into a dual-sword lunge.

I talked about the pros of this Tools, but it does have its cons. The slow animation easily leaves the player vulnerable, which is the last thing you want in a game like Sekiro. Agile enemies can sidestep, dodge if you do not time the attack correctly. Each attack consumes 2 Spirit Emblems, which is less than the Flame Vent, but spamming it quickly drains your limited pool. A good shinobi should not be afraid of striking hard.

Mist Raven

Mist Raven is the third and final Prosthetic Tool you can find within Hirata Estate. It is the easiest to miss, so be careful. Head to the Bamboo Thicket Slope idol, grappke left and swim upstream to a tree branch, follow he shore and slash through bamboo into a cave, wall-jump up to the cliff, then fight or run past the purple Lone Shadow ninja to the pagoda and grab the Mist Raven's Feathers — return them to the Sculptor at the Dilapidated Temple to craft the Mist Raven.

This Tool is underrated. It is versatile, stylish and a safe way to vanish through almost any physical attack, granting complete invulnerability. It can even reverse lightning, which is insane. If timed correctly, you will redirect the eletricity back to Genichiro/Isshin. It is to the player to choose a direction to teleport, which shows the Tool's versatility. It is the most "ninja" Tool out of the ten.

Although is very cool to use, some player view it as flashy rather than optimal. If you have mastered deflects and mikiri-counter, you are unstoppable. At the cost of two Spirit Emblems, the player must pay attention in order to not drain it all. It also requires precise timing and intentional directional input (or zero to go straight up). Use it wisely and you will fell like an anime character.

Loaded Spear

Back to the present, progress through the Ashina Outskirts and defeat Gyoubu Masataka Oniwa. Proceed to Ashina Castle and cross the main bridge to the left. Kill the two soldier there to obtain the Gatehouse Key, a necessary item in order to have the spear. By going to the Ashina Reservoir, the area where you started the game, you will see a locked gatehouse (the building you crawled under at the very start of the game) and use the key to open it. There, you will collect the Gyoubu's Broken Horn. Return to the Dilapidated Temple and speak to the Sculptor to craft the Loaded Spear.

As a long range weapon, this tool is excellent for hitting enemies that are trying to back away. It can also drag smaller enemies straight to you, letting you close the gap fast. When fighting the second phase of Guardian Ape, he will try a big overhead slam attack and, if you deflect it, he will fall. Use the pull-back twice on his neck in order to rips out the centipede, dealing absurd posture damage.

Costing only one Spirit Emblem per use, the Loaded Spear is quite strong and versatile. Be aware that this Tool is slow, making it hard to land attacks against fast enemies, like the Lone Shadows and the two Centipedes, for example. A wrong timing will leave the player vulnerable and, in the world of Sekiro, vulnerability equals death. When the Loaded Spear extends, the death draws near.

Sabimaru

This beauty is located inside Ashina Castle. It is hidden inside a chest on the bottom floor of the castle's main keep. Be aware that enemies are waiting and, if you are in the middle of the game, prepare yourself to fight a Chained Ogre. Use your grappling hook and try to fall safely, otherwise you will take fall damage. Oncle you clear the enemies (or the Ogre), head into the small adjoining room directly off the main bottom floor area. You will find a large treasure chest sitting next to an decorative set of armos. Open the chest to obtain the Sabimaru. Take the item back to the Sculptor to fit it to your prosthetic arm.

Sabimaru's gameplay pros are that its rapid multi-hit combo builds poison status quickly to deal percentage-based damage over time against high-health enemies, it shreds poison-weak foes like Okami Warriors, O'Rin, and Snake Eyes. This is a situational and fun shinobi Prosthetic Tool, rather than a universally broken weapon. With its 6-hit (or more, when upgrades) combo, Wolf will attack aggressively and pressure human-sized enemies.

If timing is poor, you can be locked into a combo chain, meaning you can easily get hit. If the enemy resists poision, there is basically no point using this Tool, and its high Spiritual Emblem cost makes it a wasteful choice agaisnt foes that are not poison weak.

Loaded Umbrella

The load the Umbrella is, without a doubt, top 3 three Prosthetic Tools in the game. In order to achieve this beauty, travel to the Old Grace Sculptor's idol in Ashina Castle. From the idol, go toward the edge and look down over the cliff. Drop down onto the roof of the building below that has a hole in the roof. Enter the building through the hole to talk to Blackhat Badger. Buy the Iron Fortress from him. If the merchant is no longer there, you can check the item lying where he stood or check the Offering Box at the Dilapidated Temple.

This masterpiece allows Wolf to block attacks from all directions and move while guarding. The deflect window is much more forgiving that using your katana. With the Projected Force skill, you can unleash a power a powerful Cross Slash directly out of your guard. A must-have tool for Apparition bosses, fire enemies, and anyone who struggles with perfectly timed sword deflects.

However, its true potential lies in the upgrades. The Phoenix's Lilac Umbrella negates Terror, shreding minibosses like Schichimen Warriors and Headless. The Suzaku's Lotus Umbrella absorbs fire, making it perfect against some enemies and even bosses, like the Demon of Hatred. A quick reminder that the tool cannot block sweeping attack, so it is a hundred percent recommendable to jumpo over those. When the Umbrella is raised, the hunter becomes the hunted.

Divine Abduction

The Divine Abduction is found in the Sunken Valley, inside the Gun Fort. To get it, you must defeat the Long-Arm Centipede Giraffe mini-boss. After the fight, loot the Large Fan from the Buddha statue in the room and give it to the Sculptor.

Unlike most of the tools, this one is not about direct damage. It spins enemies around, turning their backs to you for a free deathblow or a quick escape. It is also the only way to access the secred "Return" ending, as it can send the Divine Child of Rejuvenation to the Inner Sanctum.

WUth the Golden Vortex uphgrade, it can even blind enemies or deflect projectiles. However, it costs 2 Spirit Emblems per use and is completely useless against bosses. As a niche tool, it serves its purpose. When the wind blows, the unprepared fall.

Finger Whistle

The Finger Whistle is dropped by the Guardian Ape boss at the end of the Sunken Valley, past the Bodhisattva Valley Sculptor's Idol. After defeating the beast, take the Slender Finger item to the Sculptor to craft this tool.

As a tool entirely focused on distraction, it can draw a single enemy to your location for a stealth deathblow, or enrage beasts, making them attack their allies. It is perfect for stealth and crowd control.

However, its true purpose is tied to a specific boss. The upgrade version, called Malcontent, can be used to stun the Demon of Hatred, giving Wolf massive window to attack. Be aware that it costs three Spirit Emblems per use and has almost no affect on other bosses, besides Demon of Hatred. The whistle calls, and the beast listens.

Temporary buffs

Sugars are consumables created by the monks of the Senpou Temple. Biting into one grants Wolf a temporary combat buff that lasts 30 seconds (or 45 seconds with the Devotion latent skill). Only one Sugar effect can be active at a time; Using a new one overwrites the previous effect.

There are five Sugars in total: Ako's Sugar (Red), Yashiraku's Sugar (Dark Red), Gachiin's Sugar (Green), Ungo's Sugar (Blue) and, finally, Gokan's Sugar (Yellow).

If you are skilled, there are five Headless mini-bosses that, when defeated, drop Spiritfall items. They act as permanent, reusable versions of the Sugars. They remain in your inventory, but cost Spirit Emblems to activate.

Ako's Sugar - Attack

Ako's Sugar is a very reliable consumable in the game. It increases vitality damage dealt by 12.5% and posture damage dealt by 25%. It does not penalize your maximum health the way Yashiraku does, making it much safer and reliable.

Player generally farm this consumable around Senpou Temple by killing Senpou Monks and Lesser Centipedes. It is also possible to buy infinite Ako's Sugar from Anayama the Peddler after sending Kotaro to work for him (by giving him the Red and White Pinwheel).

Yashiraku's Sugar - High-Risk Attack

Yashiraku's Sugar is also a very reliable consumable in the game, if you already know the enemy's moveset. When consumed, it increases Vitality Damage by 25% and Posture Damage by 50%. In enchange, it halves Wolf's vitality and posture by 50%. It is high-risk and not recommended if you are still learning the patterns of the enemy in front of you.

As a strong sugar, Yashiraku's drop rate is low compared to the other consumables. Some players, instead of trying to farm, move forward to defeat the Headless. The miniboss is located in the Fountainhead Palace, below the water. If you still want to farm, try defeating apparition enemies located in the Hidden Forest, Gun Fort or Sunkey Valley.

If you look up some YouTubers, whether high-skill flashy players or speedrunners, you will notice Yashiraku is their favorite consumable. That backs up what i said in the first paragraph. High-skilled player already know what to do, so they do not back away from using this powerful buff.

Gachiin's Sugar - Stealth

Gachiin's Sugar is for players that want to go through sections without getting noticed. Even though Sekiro is more of an action game, with one versus one battles, that does not mean stealth should be underestimated. There are areas with a plethora of enemies patrolling. When that happens, there is no sugar better than Gachiin.

It is important to make something clear: even though there is a visual effect of Wolf's invisibility, it is still very easy to get spotted if you do not move like a proper Shinobi. Be as sneaky as you can, avoiding unnecessary noise, taking out enemies silently and you should be fine.

Near to the Ashina Castle Gate Fortress Sculptor's Idol, there a cliff nearby. Jump and perform an aerial deathblow on the small "rat" enemy wearing a straw hat. That an early-method to farm this Sugar. At the Shugendo Sculptor's Idol, you can find three Senpou Assassins on the cliff edge. KIll them and vacuum the drops.

Ungo's Sugar - Vitality

Ungo's Sugar is useful when you are learning new boss's patterns or trying to survive heavy physical attack, as it reduces vitality damage by 33%. It can save the player from getting one, two-shot. Although it is not an useless consumable, it is not as useful as the others. Sekiro rewards clean deflection over face-tanking hits, making Gokan's Sugar (the next one) much more useful, for example.

Senpou Monks at Senpou Temple have a chance to drop Ungo's Sugar upon defeat. However, buying is a better option, as you can acquire the consumable from two different merchants. If you want to buy, go to Fujioka the Info Broker at the dilapidated temple or the Shugendo Memorial Mob.

In sumary, if you deflect properly, your posture meter takes the hit instead of your health. making damage reduction less valuable posture buff (Gokan's Sugar) or offensive buffs, such as Ako's Sugar or Yashiraku's Sugar. It is a very situational consumable and it is very unlikely you are going to use as you progress.

Gokan's Sugar - Posture

Gokan's Sugar is pretty useful. If the player is struggling with multi-hit combos, like the Long-arm Centipedes, it may be good to use this consumable. It temporarily reduces the Posture damage by 67%. However, if your deflect timing is spot-on, your posture will never break. It is very situational rather than versatile.

The monks at Senpou Temple, Mt. Kongo have a chance to drop Gokan's Sugar upon defeat. To increase the drop rate, it is recommendable to pop a Mibu Balloon. Also, Anayama the Peddler in the Ashina Outskirts sells limited amounts of Gokan's Sugar.

Definitely more useful than the Ungo's Sugar, but it is very unlikely that you are going to use Gokan's sugar as much as you will use Gachiin or Ako. If the enemy has a multi-hit combo that quickly fills your posture bar, it is highly recommendable to use it. If it does not, there is no point using this consumable.

Bosses

Now, here comes the part that separates the men from the boys. Sekiro features fifteen main bosses, but you cannot fight all of them in a single playthrough. For example, if you choose to help Owl and abandon Kuro mid-game, you are going to fight Emma (The Gentle Blade) and Isshin Ashina.

There are three "inner versions" that you can fight, but we will not be counting them here, as it is the same boss with additional attacks. For example, Genichiro can use Sakura dance and reverse lightning. They are available to fight in Reflection of Strenght and Gauntlet of Strenght after beating the game at least once.
